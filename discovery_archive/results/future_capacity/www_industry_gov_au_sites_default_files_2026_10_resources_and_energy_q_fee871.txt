Copper-to-aluminium price ratio
Figure 11.7: Copper-to-aluminium price ratio
The copper-to-aluminium price ratio has risen from around 1–2
in the 1990s to over 4 in recent years (Figure 11.7), reflecting
stronger growth in copper demand driven by China's
industrialisation and the global energy transition. As both
metals conduct electricity and heat, the widening price gap has
renewed interest in substitution and material thrifting, mainly in
power transmission, renewable energy and electric vehicles —
where lower costs and lighter weight can favour aluminium.
Although, copper remains the preferred material in many
applications due to its superior conductivity, reliability and
space efficiency, with aluminium requiring around 1.6 times
more material to deliver equivalent conductivity. Substitution
remains constrained by technical requirements, redesign costs,
and regional factors such as tariffs and market premiums.

About this edition
The Resources and Energy Quarterly (REQ) contains forecasts for the value,
volume and price of Australia’s major resources and energy commodity
exports.
Underpinning the forecasts/projections contained in the REQ is the outlook
for global resource and energy commodity prices, demand and supply.
The forecasts/projections for Australia’s resource and energy commodity
exporters are reconciled with this global context. The global environment in
which Australia’s producers compete can change rapidly. Each edition of the
REQ factors in these changes and makes alterations to the forecasts and
projections by estimating the impact on Australian producers and the value
of their exports.
The REQ uses the IMF economic growth forecasts as the basis of its world
growth forecasts.
In this report, commodities are grouped into two broad categories, referred
to as ‘resources’ and ‘energy’. ‘Energy’ commodities comprise metallurgical
and thermal coal, oil, gas and uranium. ‘Resource’ commodities in this
report are all other mineral commodities.
Unless otherwise stated, all Australian and US dollar figures in this report are
in nominal terms. Inflation and exchange rate assumptions are provided in
Table 2.2 in the Macroeconomic outlook chapter.
Information in this edition of the REQ is current as of 20 September 2026.
