 Haynesville producers eye rising gas prices


 
 
 Tuesday April 15, 2025 | By 
 Tom Young 
 

 
 
 

 

 
 
 
 


 
 

 
 

 The Haynesville Shale remains one of the best placed sources of additional feed gas for new – albeit delayed – LNG export facilities being built on the U.S. Gulf Coast.

 The gas-focused nature of the formation could also make it one of the few shale plays in the U.S. to avoid negative economic consequences related to trade tariffs instigated by the U.S.

 The U.S. Energy Information Agency on April 10 downgraded its oil price forecast for 2025 and 2026 by $6/bl and $7bl respectively to $68/bl and $61/bl, as a series of tariff increases by the U.S. and China raised the risk of a global economic slowdown.

 Conversely, EIA forecasts Henry Hub gas prices rising by 1.9% to $4.27/mmbtu for 2025 and 2.7% to $4.6/mmbtu for 2026, increasing incentives to drill for gas. These changes are primarily driven by a faster-than-expected ramp up in exports from the Plaquemines and Corpus Christi Stage 3 LNG projects.

 Hopes for growth 

 Production has been stymied over the past 18 months by three factors: Construction delays at Golden Pass and Plaquemines LNG projects, debottlenecking delays at Freeport LNG, and high breakeven costs.

 

 Most producers need prices of $3.50/mmbtu to produce profitably, said Gordon Huddleston, President and Partner of Aethon Energy, at the Goldman Sachs Energy, CleanTech & Utilities Conference back in January.

 Henry Hub gas prices spent 2024 below that level. Operators like Tellurian, Expand Energy and Aethon Energy reduced their Haynesville rig count across 2023 and 2024.

 Golden Pass and Plaquemines are expected to come fully online before the end of 2025.

 With additional demand expected from Corpus Christi, Port Arthur and Rio Grande LNG projects, Expand Energy, for example, anticipates 11 bcf/d of additional LNG export capacity by 2027.

 Expand had eight drilling rigs across its Haynesville holdings at the end of Q4 2024 but may add a ninth in Q3 this year. The firm is guiding to a production of around 2,900 mmcfe/d from the region in 2025, up from 2,338 mmcfe/d in Q4. [i]

 Comstock Resources doubled its acreage position in the emerging Western Haynesville area to 518,000 acres last year and reduced its drilling and completion costs. Overall, the company currently holds over 800,000 net acres across the entire formation. [ii]

 “The golden age of natural gas is here and we’re on the leading edge of technology to unlock the value of the Western Haynesville,” Jay Allison, CEO of Comstock Resources, said on the firm’s Q4 results call.

 Technical challenges 

 The depth of the Haynesville makes drilling more expensive than other plays, with higher breakeven costs and greater sensitivity to gas prices.

 A 2023 EIA study found that, assuming a 10% internal rate of return, at $4/mmbtu only 13% of Haynesville acreage is profitable. At $8/mmbtu, 72% is profitable.

 With Henry Hub spot prices forecast to trade in a $4-$4.7/mmbtu range across 2025 and 2026, this would put top tier acreage firmly in profit over the next two years, although not middle and lower tier acreage.

 Huddleston said Aethon is looking for at least double-digit returns before growing production significantly.

 

 

 BP says a $4/mmbtu Henry Hub spot price would create margins for its Haynesville assets equivalent to a $70/bl oil price in the Permian. [iii] The firm currently operates two drilling rigs but may ramp up production this year.

 “On the gas side, we’re contemplating increasing rigs right now. Gas pricing is very solid as we look at the second half of 2025 and into early 2026,” said BP CEO Murray Auchincloss on the firm’s Q4 results call.

 Reductions in drilling costs starting in 2023 may further increase profitability. The bulk of Comstock Resources’ new acreage in the region is in the Western Haynesville, where average lateral lengths are shorter — and drilled footage per day lower — than in the company’s legacy assets, making production relatively more expensive. Comstock, however, says capital efficiency in the Western Haynesville is improving, with drilling costs per lateral foot lower by 33% in the latest quarter compared to the same quarter in 2022, and completion costs per lateral lower by 28%. [iv]

 Expand Energy saw a 23% improvement in average drilling costs per foot (to $335/ft) in its legacy Southwestern assets in the Haynesville during Q4 2024. [v] 

 

 Source: Expand Q4 2024 Results Presentation – Available via Evaluate Energy Documents 

 Pipeline capacity 

 Recent natural gas pipeline project completions have increased takeaway capacity, allowing for more natural gas to be transported to LNG export terminals, including Golden Pass, Plaquemines and Corpus Christi Stage 3.

 DT Midstream’s LEAP Phase 3 project expanded the existing LEAP pipeline by 0.2 bcf/d. LEAP can transport 1.9 bcf/d of natural gas from the Haynesville to Gulf Coast markets.

 TC Energy’s 1.5 bcf/d Gillis Access project, which connects with other pipelines at the Gillis Hub, also provides access to Gulf Coast LNG markets.

 “I just want to remind everybody that we do sit right adjacent to the LNG growth corridor and have 2.5 bcf/d by the end of this year of capacity to deliver to Gillis,” said Nick Dell Osso, President and CEO, on Expand Energy’s Q4 results call.

 BP is also confident about takeaway capacity. “The worry we always had on Haynesville was the takeaway rights…[but we are now] very confident that we can get the gas away if we choose to ramp up the Haynesville drilling program over the next couple of years,” said Gordon Birrell, BP’s EVP of Production and Operations, at the company’s Capital Markets Day in February.

 M&A 

 The Haynesville has a number of smaller independent operators. To date, Evaluate Energy M&A data shows that deal activity has been sporadic.

 A flurry of acquisitions occurred in 2021, including multi-billion dollar purchases by both Chesapeake Energy and Southwestern, who would later combine to form Expand. In late 2023, Tokyo Gas unit TG Natural Resources acquired 1.3 bcf/d of production from Rockcliff Energy. In March 2025, TG closed a $525mn deal to acquire a 70% interest in Chevron’s Haynesville assets.

 BP could potentially look to improve its position. In 2018, BP doubled its existing Haynesville production after acquiring BHP Billiton assets. Although the firm has not specifically mentioned further M&A in the Haynesville, its recent strategy reset [vi] aims to increase investment in oil and gas by $10bn per year through 2027, with the U.S. identified as a priority region alongside the Middle East.

 “With rising gas prices the time has come for the Haynesville,” said BP CEO Murray Auchincloss at the recent Ceraweek conference. [vii]

 Many potential acquirers may wait for a more concrete sense of higher gas prices before M&A activity gets underway in earnest.

 “I think there are still some private companies out in the Haynesville that have invested a lot of capital, and now that you’re in a good gas price situation that their business plan is to sell that – kind of like the …private companies in the Permian. [But] I think all the producers really want to get very comfortable that the gas is really needed,” said Roland Burns, CFO Comstock Resources, at the company’s Q4 results call.
