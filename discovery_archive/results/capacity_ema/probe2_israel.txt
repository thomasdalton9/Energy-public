1.3 Capacity in the Sector (GW)
Sources: Noga, IEC and the Electricity Authority’s Processing
In 2024, the sector's capacity was 24.6 GW. By 2030, the installed capacity mix is expected to change significantly: The
installed capacity of facilities producing renewable energy is expected to continue to grow to approximately 40% of the sector's
capacity. The capacity produced with coal as the primary fuel is expected to cease operation for routine usage in accordance
with Government Resolution 4080 regarding the cessation of Production Units 1-4 at the Orot Rabin Power Plants and in
accordance with the Minister of Energy's decision from 2019 and 2025 regarding the conversion of coal-fired production units to
operate on natural gas. Moreover, standalone battery storage capacity is expected to be established by the end of the decade.

2.1 Installed Capacity vs. Peak Demand (GW)
Sources: Noga and the Electricity Authority’s Processing
In 2024, the sector's production capacity was approximately 3.6 GW higher than peak demand of that year.
2030 shows an increase in expected capacity relative to peak demand. This increase is mainly due to a significant entry of storage
facilities (standalone and PV-integrated) intended to meet renewable energy goals, as well as from advancing investments to
increase competition in the sector.

2.9 Installed Capacity by Producer (GW)
Sources: Noga and the Electricity Authority’s Processing
In 2024, about 53% of the production capacity in the sector was held by IPPs, compared to only about 3% in 2012.
In 2030, although the total production capacity of the IEC is not expected to be significantly different than in 2024, the capacity
in the private market is expected to increase two-fold or more relative to the same time period.
The IEC's fuel capacity mix is expected to change significantly – from power capacity based on coal to routine use of natural
gas. This is expected to be made possible by the completion of CCGT 80 at Orot Rabin, the conversion of the coal-fired units at
Orot Rabin and Rutenberg to natural gas, and the shutdown for routine use and preservation of Units 1-4 at Orot Rabin.
Fuel capacity mix used by IPPs is expected to grow significantly, and more than double by 2030. The bulk of the increase is
expected to stem from the construction of renewable energy facilities (including facilities that integrate storage), standalone
storage facilities, and the construction of 4 CCGTs by the private market.

9.2 Installed Capacity of the IEC Divided into Technology Types (GW)
Sources: IEC and The Electricity Authority
In 2024, the Israel Electric Company operated coal-fired power plants with a total capacity of 4.8 GW. Pursuant to the Minister of
Energy's decision and Government Resolution No. 4080, the company is required to cease routine coal-fired electricity production.
As part of implementing the electricity sector reform, the Eshkol Power Plant was sold in June 2024, reducing the company's
installed production capacity by about 16%. In January 2025, CCGT 70 in Orot Rabin and Unit 1 in Rutenberg (which was converted
from coal to natural gas) became commercially operational as part of the transition to cleaner electricity production.
